# How to use this binder

Picture an old ring-binder on the kitchen counter. Tabs on the side, one
page per kind of call, a pencil tucked in the front cover. That's this
folder — except the pages never tear.

## The five-minute tour

1. **Do the walk-away math first.** Open
   [walk-away math](walk-away-math.md) and fill in two numbers: the most you
   can comfortably pay per month, and the exact offer at which you'd hang up
   happily. It costs you ten minutes, tops. This one step changes how your
   voice sounds.
2. **Pick the matching script tab.** Internet, phone, insurance,
   card-interest, or medical. Each is short enough to read on one screen —
   and out loud, in a normal voice, without rushing.
3. **Open the promise tracker.** [promise-tracker.md](promise-tracker.md)
   Same line as the call sheet. Every promise you hear gets written down
   during the call, plus the rep's first name and the date.
4. **Make the call.** Read the script. Pause a lot. Silence is a tool, not
   rudeness. When you hear a promise, read it back to them so both of you
   agree on the words: *"Just so I have it right — you're saying the
   discount will show on my April bill?"*
5. **Close the loop.** Afterward, mark the tracker. If a promise doesn't
   show up by its due date, start the [escalation ladder](escalation-ladder.md)
   at level two, still polite, now with receipts.

## Two ground rules that make everything calmer

- **Never bluff.** Everything you say in these calls is true or you don't
  say it. Honest leverage is powerful: loyalty, hardship, a competing real
  offer, your actual budget. Invented threats come back to bite you.
- **"No" is a complete sentence — but it can be soft.** See
  [Saying no kindly](saying-no.md). You can decline an add-on, a deadline, or
  a payment plan without anyone feeling scolded, and without caving either.

## If you hear something troubling

Sometimes the person on the phone will tell you something about your account
that worries you. That's the moment to slow down, write down exactly what
they said, and verify it independently before acting. This binder explains
conversations — it doesn't replace reading your real statements or talking
to a licensed professional for your specific case. **Education only; never
legal advice.**
