# The escalation ladder

Getting stuck isn't failure — it's a schedule. Move up one rung at a time,
*politely*, carrying your notes. Politeness isn't softness here; it's how
you win over the humans at every level.

## Level 0 — The call itself

Your main script, plus the read-back: promises confirmed aloud, and the
magic question — **"Can you email that with a reference number?"**
Everything above this rung works better when Level 0 left receipts.

## Level 1 — Verify, don't assume (48 hours)

- Check your statement / online account for the promised change.
- Re-read the promise tracker: did you record the rep's name, date, and
  reference number? (If not, that's the gap to fix *before* calling back.)
- Nothing landed? The next call is no longer a request, it's a follow-up:

> "Hi — I'm calling back about a promise made on [date] by [rep name],
> reference [number]. They committed [exact promise], due by [date]. It
> hasn't appeared. Can you either apply it or tell me honestly what
> happened to it?"

## Level 2 — The patient voice with receipts (1–2 weeks)

Same call, more paper. Ask for a **supervisor or retention specialist** —
calmly, and say why:

> "I'm not upset with you at all — you've been clear with me. I'd like
> someone with authority on billing adjustments, so we can settle this in
> one call instead of a fifth one."

Then walk them through your tracker line by line. Ask for the fix in
writing *this* call, not "soon."

## Level 3 — The written record (2–4 weeks)

Everything now happens on paper or email, so it dates itself:

> "Could you email me a written summary of the commitment made on [date],
> so I can reference it if it still hasn't applied by [check date]?"

Keep it warm; misentries happen. "It looks like the notes didn't transfer —
let's get the right information attached to my file." Then give them the
exact promise text and reference number.

## Level 4 — The firm-but-calm step (4–6 weeks)

For money promised and not delivered, the final internal options:

- **Billing-dispute / ombudsman / executive-care line** — most large
  providers have one; their agents have real authority. Say the word:
  "Who handles billing disputes?" then dial that line with your binder.
- **State or consumer help channel** (utility commissions, insurance
  departments, card issuers' regulators have public contact pages that are
  free to use). Ask the rep: "What's the formal dispute channel for my
  situation?" — then use exactly that channel.
- **Deliberate silence:** a week of no calls after a written dispute. Not
  a threat you say aloud — just a real pause that often moves things.

## Level 5 — The walk-away (with math already done)

You did the math before dialing, so this rung is calm rather than angry:

> "I appreciate the time you've spent today. Right now the honest answer
> is that my budget wins. I'm going to [switch / wait / choose the other
> offer]. If something changes on your end, I'd take that call happily."

Canceling is a legitimate, fully-considered choice — not a tantrum, and not
a bluff. That's why walk-away math comes first: you never have to *decide*
anything under pressure, because you decided it in your kitchen.

---

**The ladder's promise:** each rung is *nicer* than escalation-as-usual.
No yelling, no threats you can't keep. Every step is true, well
documented, and easy to defend in any later conversation — with anyone.

*Education only — this page is never legal advice; for specific disputes,
consult the proper regulator or a licensed professional.*
