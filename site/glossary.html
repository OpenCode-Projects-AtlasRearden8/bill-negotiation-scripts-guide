Glossary — plain English, no jargon

Every word here is defined the way a friend would explain it over coffee.

Agent ID — an internal number companies give their phone reps. Ask for it early in a call; it makes any later callback point at the exact person who promised you the thing.

APR (annual percentage rate) — the yearly cost of carrying a balance, as a percentage. At 25% APR, letting $1,000 sit for a year costs about $250 in interest. This is why the card-interest script exists: small APR moves are big money.

Balance transfer promo — a limited-time lower rate for moving a balance from another card. Usually has a fee (often 3–5%) and a hard end date. Read both aloud before deciding anything.

Callback / reference number — a code for your specific call. With it, "Marcus said I'd get $15 off" becomes a record someone can pull up. Without it, it's your word against the day's shift schedule.

Charity care / financial assistance — a hospital program that can reduce or forgive bills based on income. Run by the hospital itself; nonprofit hospitals are generally required to have one. Asking for the application is normal and doesn't obligate you.

EOB (explanation of benefits) — a letter from your insurer (not the hospital) explaining what was billed, what they allowed, what they paid. Not a bill. Compare it against the itemized bill line by line.

Escalation — moving a matter to someone with more authority, on purpose, politely. Our version is the escalation ladder.

Hardship (plain-words version) — "my budget genuinely can't stretch to this." You don't have to share diagnoses, job details, or documents on a discount call; honest plain words are enough to unlock what's on offer.

Itemized bill — the bill with every individual charge listed. If you only have a summary, ask for this. Hospitals send it as a matter of course.

Leverage — anything true that makes staying with you attractive: loyalty, timing, a real competing offer, your honest budget. Invented leverage is easy to detect over the phone and destroys the call.

Loyalty rate — a plan price reserved for existing customers. Often not advertised; often available if you simply ask, which is the point of these scripts.

Port-out — moving your phone number to another carrier with the same number. Saying you're "preparing a port-out" when you truly are is normal, honest leverage with phone carriers.

Proration — the odd half-charge you see when a plan changes mid-cycle. It's usually correct, just confusing. Expect one weird bill after any plan change.

Read-back — repeating each promise aloud and asking "does that match what you've entered?" It catches mishearing in the moment and commits the rep to unambiguous words.

Retention department — the team of reps authorized to keep you as a customer, usually with real discounts. Sometimes they're one transfer away; asking for "retention" by name is fine and normal.

Soft pull — a credit check the card issuer can do without your permission being needed, that doesn't affect your score. If they offer a rate cut "pending review," this is usually what they mean.

Walk-away point — the lowest offer you'd accept while still sleeping well. Decided before dialing, in walk-away math, so no phone voice gets to decide it for you.
