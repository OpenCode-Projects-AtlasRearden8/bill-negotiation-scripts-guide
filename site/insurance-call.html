Insurance call script (one page)

For denied, reduced, or "out-of-network surprise" claims. Read aloud slowly.

Before dialing

  • Have in front of you: the denial (or EOB) letter, the claim number, the date of service, and the provider's name.
  • Circle the exact reason code and its plain-English translation on the letter. Everything flows from that.
  • Walk-away note: here, "walking away" means accepting the denial silently. Instead, your line is: "I'd like to understand the appeal process."

The call

Open:

"Hi, I'm [Your Name], member ID ending in [last 4]. I'm calling about a claim that was denied [or underpaid] — claim number [number], date of service [date]. I have the letter in front of me. Could you help me understand exactly what [facility] billed and why it was denied?"

The two questions that matter:

  1. "In plain words, what is the denial reason code, and what would have to be true for this claim to be approved?"

  2. "Is this something a provider correction can fix — a coding tweak, a resubmission — or does it need a formal appeal from me?"

If a resubmission or correction may fix it:

"To make that easiest: what exactly should the office include on the resubmission, and is there a reference or ticket number I can give them?"

If you need to appeal:

"Please start that process. What's the mailing address, the form, and the deadline? And I'd like a written summary of this call, please, with the case number." (Denial letters and call summaries are normal asks — this is textbook handling, not an unusual favor.)

If it's an out-of-network surprise:

"I chose [facility] believing it was in-network. Who should I speak with about the network-status question or a balance adjustment?"

Who made the actual call — doctors decide this, not phone reps:

"Finally: is the denial saying the treatment wasn't medically appropriate, or that the paperwork wasn't sufficient? Those are different problems, and I want to ask my provider the right questions."

Saying no kindly when pushed toward giving up

"I understand this can take a few rounds. I'm not in a hurry, and I'm not giving up — I'd just like the process done correctly. What's my next step, and by when will I hear back?"

Closing — read back every promise:

"Confirming: appeal deadline [date], form [name], case number [number], expected response by [date], summary mailed to [your address on file]. Did I get all that right?"

Warm exit:

"You've been patient with me and I appreciate it. I'll keep the case number handy. Thank you."

After the call

  • Promise tracker: date, rep name, case number, every promise, every deadline. Insurance deadlines are the least forgiving of any call type in this binder — set calendar reminders the same day.
  • If no written summary arrives → escalation ladder, level 2, quotes the promise verbatim.
